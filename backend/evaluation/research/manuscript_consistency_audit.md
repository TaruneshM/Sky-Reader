## 3. Benchmark Metric Discrepancies and Resolution

During Phase 7.5.2 drafting, several discrepancies emerged between preliminary notes and post-audit summaries. Each discrepancy has been traced to its root cause and resolved:

### 3.1 Response Latency Discrepancy
- **Reported in some preliminary notes:** Baseline: ~1.70s, Tool Agent: ~21.32s, Sky Reader: ~27.81s (completed only 16.58s).
- **Reported in Phase 7.4.3 Audit & `comparative_summary.json`:** Baseline: 2,915.35 ms (2.92s), Tool Agent: 19,376.53 ms (19.38s), Sky Reader: 28,200.26 ms (28.20s).
- **Root Cause:** The 1.70s / 21.32s numbers represented unweighted means of individual sub-queries or subset categories from preliminary drafts. The 2,915.4 ms / 19,376.5 ms / 28,200.3 ms values represent the **true arithmetic mean across all 50 canonical responses** in the result store.
- **Resolution:** The canonical values (2.92s, 19.38s, 28.20s) are established as the single authoritative numbers in `authoritative_metrics.json` and updated in `results.md`, `research_tables.md`, and `fig5_latency_reliability.svg`.

### 3.2 Response Completeness Discrepancy
- **Reported in some preliminary drafts:** Baseline: 77.06%, Tool Agent: 81.33%, Sky Reader: 80.93%.
- **Reported in Phase 7.4.3 Audit & `comparative_summary.json`:** Baseline: 78.37%, Tool Agent: 83.93%, Sky Reader: 85.60%.
- **Root Cause:** The earlier values were computed prior to the Phase 7.4.3 refusal-evaluator fix, where appropriate epistemic refusals in `hallucination_resistance` and `missing_or_incomplete_data` were scored with partial completeness penalties. Symmetrically updating the refusal parser corrected the completeness scores.
- **Resolution:** The post-audit values (78.4%, 83.9%, 85.6%) are established as authoritative.

### 3.3 Numerical and Factual Denominators
- **Observation:** In preliminary drafts, varying denominators were cited for numerical variables (e.g., 72 vs. 77 vs. 79 vs. 85) and factual claims (149 vs. 121 vs. 195 vs. 180).
- **Root Cause:** 
  - *Expected Ground-Truth Variables:* The benchmark contains exactly **72 expected numerical variables** across 43 numerical cases, and **149 expected factual claims** across 50 cases. These denominators are strictly fixed across all three systems.
  - *Extracted Model Predictions:* In contrast, the number of *claims generated by the models* varied according to response verbosity (Baseline emitted 121 claims, Tool Agent emitted 195 claims, Sky Reader emitted 180 claims).
  - *Case-Level vs. Micro-Averaged Accuracy:* Case-level mean numerical accuracy averages the accuracy percentage per case ($14.34\%, 48.06\%, 34.11\%$), whereas micro-averaging divides total matched correct numbers by 72 ($12/72 = 16.67\%, 37/72 = 51.39\%, 28/72 = 38.89\%$).
- **Resolution:** `authoritative_metrics.json` and `research_tables.md` explicitly report both the case-level means and the micro-averaged counts with precise definitions.

## 4. Statistical Rigor and Multiple-Comparison Audit

### 4.1 Risk Classification Significance Correction
- **Reported Finding:** In the 11 risk-applicable test cases, Sky Reader achieved 9/11 (81.82%) accuracy compared to Tool Agent's 3/11 (27.27%) and Baseline's 0/11 (0.0%). McNemar's exact two-sided binomial test yields $p = 2 \times (0.5)^6 = 0.03125$.
- **Statistical Audit:** While $p = 0.03125$ is statistically significant at an uncorrected nominal threshold of $\alpha = 0.05$, it **does not survive a Bonferroni multiple-comparison correction** across the 12 evaluation metrics (threshold $\alpha_{\text{corr}} = 0.05 / 12 = 0.00417$).
- **Correction Applied:** The paper explicitly reports $p = 0.03125$ as *nominally significant under uncorrected exploratory testing*, and cautions that on an $N=11$ sample size, the finding must be replicated on larger severe-weather datasets.

### 4.2 Non-Significant Differences vs. Equivalence
- **Reported Finding:** Factual accuracy ($p = 0.5250$) and Completeness score ($p = 0.5750$) differences between Complete Sky Reader and Tool-Using Agent fail to reject the null hypothesis.
- **Statistical Audit:** Failing to reject $H_0$ ($p > 0.05$) does **not** prove statistical equivalence. Demonstrating equivalence requires formal Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) within prespecified equivalence bounds, which our sample size ($N=50$) is underpowered to establish for small effect sizes.
- **Correction Applied:** Language claiming "demonstrated parity" or "statistical equivalence" has been replaced with "no statistically significant difference was detected ($p > 0.50$)."

## 6. Physical Variable MAE and Dimension Validity

The legacy aggregate MAE (Baseline: 14.80, Tool Agent: 10.27, Sky Reader: 4.98) unscientifically sums errors across degrees Celsius, millimeters, and kilometers per hour.

### Mandatory Physical Decomposition (Table VII):
- **Temperature:** Baseline 7.27 °C (coverage 21/33), Tool Agent 4.03 °C (coverage 31/33), Sky Reader 5.35 °C (coverage 25/33).
- **Precipitation Accumulation:** Baseline 54.50 mm (5/12), Tool Agent 12.21 mm (7/12), Sky Reader 8.07 mm (7/12).
- **Wind Speed:** Baseline 12.20 km/h (5/10), Tool Agent 0.73 km/h (6/10), Sky Reader 6.75 km/h (4/10).
- **Precipitation Probability:** Baseline 20.75 % (4/9), Tool Agent 39.17 % (6/9), Sky Reader 0.00 % (6/9).

*Principle Enforced:* Variable-specific MAE must never be presented without its corresponding coverage ($K_{\text{matched}} / K_{\text{expected}}$). Tool Agent achieves lower MAE in temperature and wind speed because it extracts raw values without safety filtering, whereas Sky Reader trades recall for safety by refusing or bounding ambiguous telemetry.
